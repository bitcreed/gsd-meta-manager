<design_decision>
## Widen the scan rather than only correct the header

`21-VERIFICATION.md` offers the choice explicitly: widen guard six's scan to
every file under `src/`, **or** correct the header to name the file-scoping as a
third, *silent* under-detection. Widening is chosen, for a reason that is
checkable rather than aesthetic: `rtk proxy grep -rn '\.finish(' src/` returns
`src/driver/run.rs:993` (inside `finish_run`, the sanctioned helper) plus five
lines in `src/journal/mod.rs` at `:2814`, `:3057`, `:3135`, `:3166` and `:3371`,
all of which are **past** that file's column-zero `mod tests {` at `:2101` and
are therefore already excluded by guard six's existing per-file
`test_region_start` boundary. So the widened scan is green today with **no
allowlist entry needed beyond `finish_run` itself** — the property already holds
tree-wide and was merely unchecked. Widening costs one iteration and converts a
silent gap into a checked one.

The header is corrected **as well**, because widening removes one
under-approximation and not the other. What remains after this task: the
line-marker production/test boundary, the line-comment-only filter, and the call
spelling. Each must be named individually with its own failure direction, rather
than summarised. That is the specific thing `21-REVIEW.md` faulted.

## Why the `CommandSource` guard keys on variant spellings

`src/driver/run.rs:614` declares a **second, unrelated** `enum CommandSource`
with variants `Fixed` and `Routed`, constructed at `:2598-2602` and matched at
`:3271-3272`. A naive scan for the bare type name would report those as
offenders. The three needles are therefore the full variant spellings with an
opening parenthesis: the `Command` and `Goal` spellings do not exist on
`run.rs`'s enum at all, and `run.rs` writes its `Routed` variant with a brace
rather than a parenthesis because it is a struct variant. Verified by reading
both declarations; the executor must re-verify before trusting it, because the
guard's soundness rests on that difference.

Renaming one of the two types is the better long-term answer and is **out of
this round's scope** — `21-11-PLAN.md`'s `<assumption_delta_decision>` records
it as accepted debt and names what would force the rename. This guard is written
so that the rename would make it *simpler*, not so that it depends on the
collision persisting.

## Why the legacy-record doc is reworded rather than deleted

The doc paragraph exists to answer a real question a reader has: *"records
written by an earlier dev build carry `fnv1a64:` plan digests — is there a
migration?"* The answer is still "no migration is needed", so the paragraph
stays. Only its **reason** changes, from a record path production cannot take to
the two facts that are true: no recorded `ApprovedPlan` is ever deserialised and
re-checked, and the legacy value a user could actually be holding is a token on
argv that `parse_approval_token` refuses by name. Deleting the paragraph would
leave the reader's question unanswered and invite someone to re-derive the wrong
answer.
</design_decision>
